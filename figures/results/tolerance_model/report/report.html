The short answers to your questions

How were RSA and the structural annotations included in the training data? They were attached to every residue by a lookup on (gene, residue number) in §7–§8, giving one big table with one row per residue. A variant then copies the row of the residue it sits on (section 4, Figure 8). They were inputs to two models: the tree model that predicts tolerance (14g), and the variant model (14h, §16). They were deliberately not inputs to the sequence-only networks of your original design, which see only the protein sequence.
How were ESM's weights and scores used? In three ways (Figure 7). (1) Scores: log-probabilities from ESM-1v became four numbers per variant. (2) Representations: ESM-1v's internal description of each residue, 1,280 numbers, fed the networks that predict tolerance. (3) Fine-tuned weights (LoRA on ESM-2) are planned for Biowulf but haven't been run. ESM's own weights have not been changed in any model trained so far.
How were ClinVar clinical significance and gnomAD allele frequency used? The ClinVar classification is the answer the variant model learns, and the yardstick for every accuracy number. ClinVar variant positions (any classification) feed the ClinVar side of the tolerance score. gnomAD variant positions feed the gnomAD side. The gnomAD allele count is used only as a filter (a variant must be seen at least twice). The gnomAD allele frequency is not used by any model (Figure 2).
What features was the model actually trained on? The final §16 model used 42 numbers per variant in seven groups, listed one by one in section 7, plus "was this value missing?" flags. The models of your original design used only the sequence: amino-acid tokens, or ESM's 1,280-number representations.
Which accuracy metrics improved? For the variant model, the main metric is the AUROC: the chance that a random benign variant gets a more benign score than a random pathogenic one. On the untouched test genes it rose from 0.62 (ESM-1v alone) to 0.89, and where ESM is unsure from 0.55 (chance) to 0.88. For the tolerance-predicting models, the metric is the within-gene Spearman correlation. It rose from 0.02 (ESM alone) to 0.125 at best, against a maximum possible of 0.39 (section 9, Figure 29).

The running example

Most diagrams use one gene, GLRA1 (the α1 subunit of the glycine receptor, 449 residues), and one variant in it, p.A300P: alanine 300 replaced by proline, classified as pathogenic in ClinVar. GLRA1 is a training gene, so its data were used to fit the models but not to judge them.

1. Two ways to build a tolerance model

A machine-learning model is a formula with adjustable numbers (its weights). It is given inputs (also called features: everything it is allowed to look at) and produces an output. While it trains, its output is compared with the correct answer (the target), and the weights are adjusted to make the two agree better. After training, the model is tested on data it has never seen. The choice of target decides what the model becomes.

Design A: predict tolerance (the original goal)

The observed tolerance score (section 3) can only be computed where human variants have been seen, and it is noisy. A model that predicts it from the protein sequence alone could give a tolerance value for every residue of any protein, smoothing out the noise. §11 turned every protein into model input, §12 built the model (ESM-2 with a small "head" network), and §13 tested the code on this laptop. §14 then asked whether the target can be learned at all.

Design B: predict the clinical label (what §16 tested)

Your underlying hypothesis is that human population data mark constraint that evolution (ESM) misses. The most direct test of that hypothesis is at the level of single variants: put the human-variant evidence and the evolutionary evidence side by side, and see whether together they separate known pathogenic from known benign variants better than ESM alone.

Why the notebook moved from A to B in §14

  • The tolerance target is mostly noise at the level of single residues. Built twice from two random halves of the variants, the two versions barely agree. Even a perfect model could reach a correlation of only about 0.39 with it (section 3).
  • The best Design A model reached 0.125, about a third of that ceiling (section 6).
  • As a way to classify variants, predicted tolerance did worse than ESM alone (AUROC 0.50–0.79 vs 0.94 on the validation genes), because one tolerance value per residue can't tell a mild swap from a drastic one at the same position.
  • Design B worked well: combining tolerance with ESM lifted the AUROC well above ESM alone (section 7). So 14i recommended making it the main product, and §16 tested it.
In plain words Your original model predicts how tolerant each residue is. The tested model predicts whether a specific variant is benign, and uses the tolerance score as one of its clues. Design A has been built and tried, but not tested on the untouched genes, and its GPU fine-tuning hasn't been run. Section 9 shows how the two designs can be reconciled.

3. Step 1: turning variants into a tolerance score (§3)

The idea: positions where healthy people carry many missense variants are probably tolerant of change, and positions where disease reports cluster are probably intolerant. Figure 3 builds the score for one gene, GLRA1 (a glycine-receptor subunit), which is used as the running example throughout this guide.

The steps in detail

  1. Choose which variants count. gnomAD variants seen in at least 2 people (a variant seen once may be a sequencing error or one person's private variant). On the X and Y chromosomes, a gnomAD variant counts only if a man (with a single X) or a homozygous woman carries it. A variant seen only in women with a second, normal X could be a hidden recessive disease variant. All ClinVar missense variants count, whatever their classification.
  2. Weight each variant by how drastic it is (Figure 4, right). Swapping alanine for serine is mild; tryptophan for cysteine is drastic. In ordered regions the BLOSUM62 table supplies this weight, in disordered regions the Grantham distance. If several variants sit at one residue, the most drastic one counts.
  3. Spread each variant over its neighbours (Figure 4, left). Nearby residues usually share a fate, so each weight is spread with a bell-shaped curve: wide (±31 residues) in ordered regions, narrow (±5) in disordered ones, where neighbours matter less. AIUPred's disorder score decides which applies.
  4. Subtract. Each smoothed density is scaled to run from 0 to 1 within the gene, then tolerance = gnomAD − ClinVar, so it runs from −1 to +1.
  5. Centre. Each gene's median is subtracted. This removes differences in overall level between genes (some genes simply have more ClinVar submissions), so the target describes which parts of a protein are more or less tolerant than the rest of that protein. This centred value, tolerance_v4_centered, is the target of Design A.

How reliable is the tolerance score? The noise ceiling (14a)

To see how much of the score is signal and how much is chance, the variants were split randomly into two halves, and the score was built twice, once from each half. If the score were pure signal, the two versions would be nearly identical.

From that agreement, the reliability of the full score is about 0.15, and the noise ceiling is √0.15 ≈ 0.39: no model, however good, could correlate more than about 0.39 with this target, because the target itself is mostly noise at single-residue resolution. That is the yardstick for Design A (section 6). 14g later found a recipe with a much higher ceiling (0.61): building the ClinVar side from pathogenic variants only (section 6).

4. Step 2: evidence about each residue (§4, §7, §8)

ESM: what evolution says about each position

ESM is a protein language model. It was trained on tens of millions of natural protein sequences to fill in hidden amino acids, and in doing so learned which amino acids fit where. For each residue it gives a probability for every one of the 20 amino acids. Positions where evolution tolerates almost no change get a narrow, confident distribution; tolerant positions get a spread-out one.

The four ESM numbers, all computed once in §4 and cached:

NamePerMeaning
esm_llrvariantlog P(new amino acid) − log P(original). Negative = the swap is unexpected; the more negative, the worse
esm_wt_lpresiduelog-probability of the amino acid that is there
esm_entropyresiduehow spread out ESM's probabilities are (low = a tightly constrained position)
esm_mean_llrresiduethe average LLR of all 19 possible swaps at this residue (one number per residue)

The three ways ESM was used

A representation is ESM's internal description of a residue: a list of 1,280 numbers that summarises what ESM has inferred about that residue from the whole protein around it. It is far richer than the four scores, but it isn't readable directly. It becomes useful only when another model learns how to translate it into the quantity you care about, here tolerance.

Structure, annotations and disorder

  • RSA (relative solvent accessibility): the share of a residue's surface exposed to water, from 0 (buried in the core) to 1 (fully exposed). It was computed from the best experimental chain where one exists (PDB), otherwise from the AlphaFold model, and left empty where AlphaFold's confidence (pLDDT) is below 70. Available for 61% of residues; the rest are mostly disordered.
  • Interfaces: whether the residue touches another chain in any experimental structure (from PDBe-KB): a copy of itself (iface_homo), a different protein (iface_hetero), or either (iface_any).
  • Annotations: yes/no flags for domains, repeats, motifs, binding regions, linear interacting peptides, PTM sites, disulfides, ligand and active sites, conserved sites, coiled coils, transmembrane segments and signal peptides, plus a count of how many apply.
  • Disorder: AIUPred's disorder and binding scores, and MobiDB's curated disorder. Conservation: MobiDB's PSI-BLAST conservation score, available for 53% of residues.

How it all comes together: one row per residue, one row per variant

Every source uses its own numbering (UniProt, PDB, AlphaFold), so §7 and §8 first translate everything onto the MANE sequence's numbering. Then every value is a column in one table, residue_df, with one row per residue: 780,750 rows in all. A variant needs only a lookup: which gene, which residue? This is how RSA and the annotations "entered the training data".

5. How accuracy is measured

Data the model has never seen

A model can look perfect on the data it learned from simply by memorising it (overfitting). Accuracy therefore always has to be measured on data kept aside. §10 split the genes three ways, keeping families of related genes (clusters: similar sequences or the same PANTHER family) together, so a test gene never has a close relative in training:

SplitProteinsResiduesUsed for
Training854618,540learning the weights
Validation10779,309comparing models and choosing settings (§14)
Test10778,366one final, untouched check (§16)

Within the training genes, cross-validation gives every variant an honest score: the data are cut into 5 parts by cluster, the model is fitted on 4 parts and scores the 5th, and this repeats until every part has been scored (Figure 14, right).

For Design A: within-gene Spearman correlation

Design A predicts a whole track per protein. The question is whether the predicted track rises and falls where the real one does. The Spearman correlation compares the ranks: +1 means the residues are in exactly the same order, 0 means no relation, −1 means reversed. It is computed within each gene, and the median over the validation genes is reported. The noise ceiling (0.39) is the best possible score.

For Design B: the AUROC

Design B gives each variant a score. Take every pair of one truly benign and one truly pathogenic variant, and count how often the benign one gets the more benign score. That share is the AUROC (area under the ROC curve). 0.5 is coin-flipping; 1.0 is perfect. It doesn't depend on choosing a cut-off, and it is the same as the area under the curve traced out by sliding a cut-off from one end of the scores to the other.

Three versions of the AUROC

  • All variants: the standard number, pooled over genes.
  • Within genes: the average AUROC inside each gene with at least 3 pathogenic and 3 benign variants. This is the clinical question: in a gene we know, is this variant worse than that one? Pooling can hide or exaggerate differences (Figure 11), so both are reported.
  • Where ESM is unsure: only the variants ESM-1v calls mild (its LLR is above the median for benign training variants). This is where human data were expected to help most.

How sure are we? Bootstrap ranges

The test genes are just one sample of genes; a different sample would give somewhat different numbers. The bootstrap estimates how much: draw the test genes again at random, with replacement, 1,000 times, recompute the score each time, and keep the middle 95% of the results as the 95% range. Whole genes are resampled, because variants in the same gene aren't independent. To compare two models, the difference is recomputed on each resample (a paired bootstrap). A comparison "passes" when the whole range is above 0.

Decisions and honest probabilities

The AUROC measures ranking. To make a call, a cut-off is needed: for example, call a variant benign when P(benign) ≥ 0.5. Then the useful numbers are the share of pathogenic variants caught, the share of benign variants correctly cleared, and the overall accuracy. Calibration asks whether a stated probability can be taken at face value: of the variants given P(benign) ≈ 0.8, are about 80% really benign? The Brier score (mean squared error of the probabilities; 0 is perfect) summarises it. Both are shown in section 8.

Every Design A model gets the same target (the centred tolerance of each residue in the training genes) and is scored on the validation genes by within-gene Spearman correlation. They differ in what they see.

How the networks were trained

  • Loss: the Huber loss, a measure of how far the predicted track is from the target that is forgiving of the occasional large miss. Padding (empty space after short proteins) never counts.
  • Epochs: each training protein contributes one random window of up to 512 residues per pass; 16 windows are processed at a time.
  • Early stopping: 10% of the training clusters are set aside. After every epoch the model predicts them, and training stops when that score stops improving, keeping the best epoch. That is the point where the model starts to memorise rather than learn (overfitting).
  • Seeds: each network was trained 3 times from different random starting weights, because small networks vary from run to run.

Results

  • ESM's pre-training matters: networks trained from scratch on ~850 proteins reached 0.03–0.05; the same networks on frozen ESM reached 0.09–0.125.
  • Context helps a little: the BiLSTM, which reads the whole window in both directions, was the best of each group.
  • The best model reached 0.125, a third of the 0.39 ceiling.
  • Predicted tolerance is a weak variant classifier (AUROC 0.50–0.79), well below ESM-1v's LLR (0.94): one value per residue can't distinguish a mild swap from a drastic one at the same position.

Adding structure and annotations to predict tolerance (14g, part 1)

This is where RSA, interfaces and annotations were tried as inputs to Design A. A tree model got all 97 per-residue numbers, and each group was tested alone and removed in turn.

All groups together reached 0.126, essentially the same as the best ESM head. Structure and annotations add nothing reliable to this target. One likely reason is that the target is so noisy that small, real effects disappear.

A better target (14g, part 2)

Building the ClinVar side from pathogenic variants only (instead of every ClinVar entry, VUS and benign included) raised the ceiling from 0.37 to 0.61, the probe from 0.10 to 0.32, and the held-out AUROC of the track itself from 0.79 to 0.84. Keeping gnomAD variants seen only once made the target more reproducible but less useful. This is the most promising lead for your original design (section 9).

Missing values (for example, no structure for a residue) are filled with the feature's median, and a "was it missing?" flag is added so the model can learn whether missingness matters. Every feature is then standardised (shifted and scaled to mean 0, spread 1), so the weights are comparable.

Hiding each variant's own residue

If a variant's own position fed its human-variant features, a pathogenic variant would partly vouch for itself, and so would a second pathogenic substitution at the same residue. So the residues with labelled variants are dealt into 5 groups, and the features of a variant in group k come from tracks rebuilt without any variant at group-k residues.

The model: logistic regression

Training means choosing the 42 weights (plus those for the missing-value flags) that make the training labels as likely as possible, with a small penalty on very large weights. It takes a fraction of a second. Gradient-boosted trees, a small neural network (MLP) and two networks that read raw variant positions (window CNN and BiLSTM) were also tried in 14h, all with the same cross-validation by cluster (Figure 14, right).

What the model relies on

Weights of features that move together (such as the ClinVar counts and the gene-wide ClinVar rate) can't be read one at a time: the model can trade them against each other. Read the top of the list as "what the model leans on", not as separate causes.

A closer look (descriptive, after the test)

What the test shows
  • The model works on genes nobody chose (Q1), and helps most where evolution alone is at chance (Q3). Human variant data add to evolution in new genes (Q4).
  • The size of the Q1 gap partly reflects this test set. Three genes hold half of it, and ESM struggles in two of them. In typical genes the gain is about +0.05 AUROC.
  • The ranking is good, but the probabilities aren't yet trustworthy for new genes. Read them as ranks or recalibrate them. A plausible reason for the RYR1 miss: its expert-classified variants mostly concern malignant hyperthermia, which causes no symptoms without anaesthetic triggers, so carriers can look "healthy" in gnomAD.

Getting back to your original goal

Your goal was a model whose prediction is the tolerance score. Three routes lead there, and they can be combined:

  1. Train Design A on the better target. Rebuild the target with the ClinVar side from pathogenic variants only (CLINVAR_GROUPS = ("pathogenic",) in §3), retrain the ESM heads on this laptop, then optionally run the LoRA fine-tuning on Biowulf (§15). The result is a sequence-only tolerance predictor with three times more learnable signal than now. It would need its own pre-registered test on the test genes. That is legitimate, because no Design A choice was based on the §16 result, but it should be reported as a separate analysis.
  2. Turn Design B into a tolerance score. The final model can score all 19 possible substitutions at every residue: ESM, structure, annotations and nearby human variants exist for any residue. Averaging the 19 values of P(benign) gives one model-predicted tolerance per residue. It is available everywhere, including residues where no variant has been seen, and it comes from a model whose variant-level accuracy has been checked on untouched genes. This is the quickest way to "a prediction score that determines tolerance". The per-residue average itself would still need its own check, for example against the observed track.
  3. Combine them. Feed Design A's predicted tolerance into the variant model as one more feature (§16h), so sequence-learned tolerance and observed human variation inform each other.
In plain words Your original idea, predicting tolerance, is still alive. It was held back by a noisy target, not by the model. With the pathogenic-only target it becomes three times more learnable. Meanwhile, the variant model can already be turned into a per-residue tolerance score, built from a model whose accuracy has been checked on genes nobody chose.

From genome to damaging missense variants

What to say: "Each of us carries thousands of protein-changing variants. Truncating variants are usually clearly damaging, but most protein-changing variants are missense: a single amino acid swapped. Most of those are tolerated, and the damaging ones are hard to predict. That's the gap this project works on."

Check the numbers before presenting The flowchart has no citation, and two of its numbers are lower than the standard reference. The 1000 Genomes Project (2015) reports that a typical genome differs from the reference at 4.1–5.0 million sites, with 149–182 protein-truncating and 10,000–12,000 peptide-altering sites. Compare that with "~2 million variants" and "~500 missense variants" on the poster. If your numbers come from a different definition (for example rare variants only), be ready to say which; otherwise consider updating them. "~100 loss-of-function" is in line with published estimates.

Evolutionary Scale Modelling

  • What ESM is: a protein language model trained on about 250 million natural protein sequences. During training it learns to predict amino acids that have been hidden. The score for a variant is the LLR: how much less likely ESM finds the new amino acid than the original. Strongly negative means "evolution rarely allows this".
  • What the histogram shows: ClinVar pathogenic variants get lower (more negative) scores than benign or gnomAD variants. This is Brandes et al.'s figure, made with ESM-1b. Your model uses ESM-1v, a sibling trained specifically for variant effects. If asked, the two behave similarly.
  • Your own numbers, held-out variants (I1, made today but not on the poster):
    • mean LLR: gnomAD −5.77, ClinVar benign −4.44, VUS −5.53, pathogenic −10.53;
    • ESM-1v separates gnomAD from pathogenic variants well (AUROC 0.80);
    • it cannot tell VUS from gnomAD variants (0.48). That is the opening for human variation.

13. Methods panels

Calculating tolerance scores

  1. Collect the variants: missense variants from gnomAD v4.1 (seen at least twice) and from ClinVar (any classification).
  2. Weight each variant by how drastic its swap is, which is the lollipop length. Structured regions use BLOSUM62, which reflects which swaps evolution tolerates in folded proteins. Unstructured regions (AIUPred ≥ 0.5) use the Grantham distance, which measures how chemically different two amino acids are.
  3. Smooth the weights with a binomial window: wide in structured regions (w = 31), where neighbours in the fold share constraint, and narrow in unstructured regions (w = 5), where function sits in short motifs.
  4. Scale and subtract: each density is scaled 0–1 within the gene, and tolerance = gnomAD − ClinVar. Above 0 (blue) means more healthy variation than clinical reports (tolerated); below 0 (red) means intolerant.

Wording: a binomial window puts most of its weight near the centre. 97% of a w = 31 window lies within ±8 residues, and 98% of a w = 5 window within ±3. So "w = 31" is the window's full reach, not its effective width.

The model table

16. Questions to expect

About the idea

What exactly does the model predict? For each variant (one amino-acid change at one position), the probability that it would be classified benign rather than pathogenic. It is trained on ClinVar labels. It does not output the tolerance score: tolerance is one of its 42 inputs.
How is your tolerance score different from existing constraint scores? Scores such as the missense tolerance ratio or regional missense constraint use population variation only, comparing what is seen in gnomAD with what would be expected. This score differs in three ways:
  • it subtracts the density of clinically reported variants;
  • it weights each variant by how drastic the swap is;
  • it adapts the window to structured vs disordered regions.
A head-to-head comparison with those scores hasn't been done yet.
How does it compare with AlphaMissense, REVEL or CADD? Not compared yet. ESM-1v was the planned baseline because it is the evolution-only starting point the hypothesis is about. Several of those tools were trained or calibrated with clinical or population labels, so a fair comparison has to remove overlapping variants. It's a natural next step.
Why postsynaptic proteins? They are highly conserved, densely annotated (SynGO, 1,082 proteins) and central to neurodevelopmental disorders, with many variants still unclassified. The method itself isn't specific to them.

About the data

Isn't it circular to use ClinVar both in the features and as the label? That's the main risk, and there are three safeguards:
  • Residue hiding: a variant's own residue is hidden when its human-variant features are built.
  • Gene-family splits: related genes are always kept in the same split.
  • Untouched test genes: the final test used 85 genes that played no part in any choice, with the plan fixed beforehand.
What remains is that ClinVar density partly reflects where labs look, which is why the results are presented as a ranking and the VUS question is still open.
Which variants were used?
  • Labels: missense variants rated pathogenic/likely pathogenic or benign/likely benign in ClinVar, with at least one review star.
  • Tolerance score: all ClinVar missense entries (VUS included) on the ClinVar side, and gnomAD v4.1 missense variants with an allele count of at least 2 on the other. On chrX, a gnomAD variant counts only if a male or a homozygous female carries it.
  • Not used: allele frequency itself is not a feature.
Where do the structures come from? For each protein, the best-covering experimental structure via PDBe, mapped to the MANE sequence. Where there's none, the AlphaFold model is used, with low-confidence residues (pLDDT < 70) left blank. Interfaces come from PDBe-KB, which pools contacts across all PDB entries.
Why two window sizes and two substitution tables? In folded regions, residues that are far apart in sequence pack together in 3D, so constraint spreads along the chain. That calls for a wide window and BLOSUM62, which reflects the swaps evolution allows in folded proteins. In disordered regions, function sits in short motifs, so the window is narrow and severity is a plain chemical distance (Grantham).
Could ancestry bias in gnomAD matter? Yes. gnomAD over-represents European ancestry, so variants that are common in under-represented populations can be missed, and regions can look less tolerant than they are. That's a real limitation.

About the model

Why logistic regression and not deep learning? In cross-validation, logistic regression (0.961) matched or beat gradient-boosted trees (0.958), an MLP (0.959), a BiLSTM (0.950) and a CNN (0.943). When a simple model does as well, the limit is the information in the data. The simple model is also easier to check and to interpret.
What does the model rely on? Its largest weights, per standard deviation of each feature:
  • ESM-1v LLR: +1.53 (towards benign);
  • gnomAD variants within ±15 residues: +1.07;
  • ClinVar variants within ±15 residues: −0.99 (towards pathogenic);
  • then conservation (−0.90), interfaces (−0.49), exposure (+0.44) and swap severity (−0.40).
The tolerance score itself gets +0.29, because the raw counts carry the same information and share the credit.
Does structure add anything beyond ESM? Yes. In cross-validation, adding each group to ESM's scores improved the AUROC:
  • structure: +0.036;
  • disorder and conservation: +0.040;
  • human variants nearby: +0.029.
All three gains are clearly above zero. They overlap because all three mark constrained regions.

About the results

ESM-1v usually scores around 0.9. Why only 0.62 here? The test set is dominated by three genes: RYR1, ABL1 and VHL hold 52% of its variants. ESM gives pathogenic variants in RYR1 and VHL mild scores. In the other 82 test genes ESM-1v scores 0.91 and the model 0.96, the same gap as in cross-validation (0.906 vs 0.961).
Is the improvement statistically significant? Yes, for the three main questions, with 95% ranges from resampling whole genes:
  • all variants: +0.27 (+0.05 to +0.45);
  • where ESM is unsure: +0.34 (+0.18 to +0.47);
  • ESM + tolerance vs ESM alone: +0.21 (+0.02 to +0.30).
The within-gene comparison (+0.16) wasn't significant, because only 8 test genes had enough variants of both kinds.
What does "pre-registered" mean here? Before the test genes were scored, the plan was saved with a fingerprint (9d090042fbe2): the primary model, the comparisons and the pass criteria. It was then run once. It's an internal registration, not a public registry, so say "fixed in advance" if asked.
Are the probabilities reliable? Not yet. At P(benign) ≥ 0.5, 97% of benign test variants were classified correctly but only 52% of pathogenic ones, mostly because of RYR1. Read the output as a ranking within a gene, or recalibrate it.

Limits and next steps

Can it find which VUS are pathogenic? Not measured yet. The test used confidently classified variants, which are the clearer cases, so expect lower performance on VUS. The cleanest test is a time split: take a ClinVar release from a few years ago, rebuild the features from it, and check whether VUS that were later reclassified as pathogenic ranked higher than those later called benign. Lab-measured effects (deep mutational scans in MaveDB) and de novo variants in patients are other options.
What about genes with few known variants? The human-variant features need variants nearby. Where few have been seen, those features carry little information and the model leans on ESM and structure.
What's next?
  1. The VUS time-split test.
  2. Calibration.
  3. A comparison with AlphaMissense and other tools.
  4. Scoring all 19 possible swaps at every residue to turn the model into a per-residue predicted tolerance.
  5. Your original design: predicting tolerance from sequence, with the cleaner pathogenic-only target (section 9).
Phylogeny: why is regulatory DNA so poorly conserved? Regulatory elements turn over quickly in evolution, and most elements in the Regulatory Build aren't deeply conserved. Bases that don't align count as not identical, so many elements score near 0 outside mammals. Coding DNA is far more conserved, and protein more again, because many DNA changes don't alter the amino acid.

TermMeaning
AIUPreda predictor of disordered (unstructured) protein regions and of disordered binding sites, from sequence alone
AlphaFolda model that predicts protein structures; its database has a predicted structure for every human protein
AUROCthe chance that a random benign variant gets a more benign score than a random pathogenic one. 0.5 = chance, 1 = perfect
BLOSUM62, Granthamtables of how different two amino acids are (evolutionary substitution rates; chemical distance)
Bootstrap, 95% rangeresampling the genes with replacement many times and keeping the middle 95% of the recomputed results
Calibration, Brier scorewhether stated probabilities match observed frequencies; the Brier score is their mean squared error (0 = perfect)
Centringsubtracting each gene's median tolerance, so each track is measured from its own typical level
Clustera group of related genes (similar sequence or same PANTHER family), always kept in the same split
Cohen's dthe difference between two means in units of their standard deviation; 0.8 or more is usually called large
Cross-validationfitting on 4/5 of the data and scoring the other 1/5, rotating, so every item is scored by a model that never saw it
Early stoppingending training when the score on set-aside data stops improving
Epochone pass over all the training data
ESM-1v, ESM-2protein language models (650 million weights) trained on natural protein sequences
Feature, inputa number the model is allowed to look at
Fine-tuning, LoRAcontinuing to train a pre-trained model on a new task; LoRA trains small add-on matrices instead of all its weights
Frozena pre-trained model whose weights are kept fixed while something on top of it is trained
Identity, similarityidentity counts identical amino acids (or bases); similarity also counts conservative swaps (positive BLOSUM62 score)
Interface residuean exposed residue that touches another protein chain in an experimental structure (here from PDBe-KB)
KS statistic (D)the largest vertical gap between two cumulative distributions: 0 = identical, 1 = no overlap
LLRlog-likelihood ratio: log P(new amino acid) − log P(original), from ESM. Negative = unexpected swap
Logistic regressiona weighted sum of features passed through an S-shaped curve to give a probability
Lossa number measuring how far the model's output is from the target; training makes it smaller
MANEone agreed reference transcript per protein-coding gene (NCBI and Ensembl); all residue numbers here use it
Noise ceilingthe highest correlation any model could reach with a noisy target
Orthologuethe same gene in another species, descended from one ancestral gene
Overfittingmemorising the training data instead of learning rules that carry over to new data
pLDDTAlphaFold's confidence in its structure model at a residue (0–100)
Pre-registrationfixing the analysis plan (models, comparisons, pass criteria) before seeing the test data
RepresentationESM's internal description of a residue: 1,280 numbers summarising its context
Residue hidingbuilding a variant's human-variant features without any variant at its own residue
RSArelative solvent accessibility: the share of a residue's surface exposed to water
Spearman correlationagreement between two rankings: +1 same order, 0 unrelated, −1 reversed
SynGOan expert-curated database of synapse genes; the source of the 1,082 postsynaptic proteins
Targetthe answer a model learns to produce
Validation / test setgenes held out from training: validation for choosing between models, test for one final check
VUSvariant of uncertain significance: a ClinVar entry with no pathogenic or benign call
