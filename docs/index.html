The question

Can human variation add what evolution misses?

Each of us carries thousands of variants that change one amino acid in a protein (missense variants). Most are harmless, and the damaging ones are hard to recognise. Protein language models such as ESM-1v learn from millions of natural protein sequences which amino acids evolution allows at each position, and they already separate many pathogenic variants from those seen in healthy people.

This project asks whether two more kinds of evidence help:

  • Human population variation: where healthy people carry variants (gnomAD) and where clinically reported variants cluster (ClinVar), turned into a per-residue tolerance score.
  • Protein structure: whether a residue is buried or exposed, folded or disordered, at an interface or in a known domain or motif.
Design A (the original goal)

A model that predicts the tolerance score from the protein sequence, so the prediction becomes the tolerance score. It was built and tried, but its target turned out to be too noisy residue by residue. See Design A.

Design B (what the poster tests)

A model that predicts each variant's ClinVar label (pathogenic or benign). The tolerance score is one of its 42 inputs. It was chosen by cross-validation and tested once on untouched genes. See experiments 3 and 4.

How the genes were split

Models are judged on genes they were not trained on. Genes are split by family: genes with at least 40% sequence identity or from the same PANTHER family always land in the same split, so a model can't score a test gene by recognising a close relative from training. The validation genes were used to compare and choose models; the test genes were used once, at the end.

Genes, residues and labelled variants in each split

Labelled = ClinVar pathogenic / likely pathogenic or benign / likely benign with at least one review star. 12 genes without a usable tolerance score were left out of the split.

Examples

Change the recipe

The official recipe (notebook 05, §3): each variant is weighted by how drastic its swap is (BLOSUM62 where the residue is folded, Grantham distance where it is disordered), the most severe swap at a residue counts, the weights are smoothed with a binomial window (w = 31 in folded, w = 5 in disordered regions; the window spans 2w + 1 residues), both densities are scaled 0–1 within the gene, and tolerance = gnomAD − ClinVar.

Experiment 1

Does the tolerance score recognise variants it has never seen?

10% of all variants (62,942) were set aside. The tolerance score was rebuilt from the other 90% and read off at each held-out variant (62,884 of them, in 1,069 genes with a usable score). If the score captures real constraint, held-out pathogenic variants should fall in low-tolerance regions and gnomAD variants in high ones. ESM-1v's score of the same variants is shown for comparison.

Read with care

The ClinVar side of the tolerance score counts every ClinVar entry, VUS included. A held-out variant was not counted itself, but its ClinVar neighbours were, and clinical labs submit many variants from the same genes and regions. So part of the gap between ClinVar and gnomAD variants is built in. The cleanest comparison is pathogenic vs gnomAD (AUROC 0.90 for tolerance vs 0.80 for ESM-1v), and the strongest test is experiment 4, on genes never used at all.

"As on the poster" uses the poster panel's subset: held-out variants found in one database only, at residues where another substitution was observed.

Experiment 2

Where in the structure are pathogenic variants?

Each residue gets a structural class from its relative solvent accessibility (RSA, the share of its surface exposed to water) in the best experimental structure, or in the AlphaFold model when there is none: core (RSA < 0.2), boundary (0.2–0.4) or surface (> 0.4). Exposed residues that touch another protein chain in any PDB entry (PDBe-KB) are interface residues.

Share of held-out ClinVar variants that are pathogenic, by structural class

Held-out pathogenic and benign variants (at least one review star) in all genes, pooled. Ligand-binding residues (PDBe-KB and UniProt) overlap the other classes.

Exposed residues tolerate change, unless they form an interface or bind a ligand: then they are as constrained as the core. This is why the model gets structure and interface features, and why the tolerance score uses a wider window where a protein is folded (neighbours in the fold share constraint) than where it is disordered (function sits in short motifs).

Experiment 3

Choosing a model by cross-validation

Eight ways to predict the ClinVar label were compared on the 15,339 labelled variants of the training and validation genes (5,449 pathogenic, 9,890 benign), with 5-fold cross-validation that keeps gene families together. Every human-variant feature was rebuilt with the variant's own residue hidden. A simple logistic regression on the 42 features did as well as any neural network, so it was fixed in advance as the one model to test on the untouched genes.

What each kind of data adds

Gradient-boosted trees were refitted on each feature group alone, and with each group added to the ESM scores.

Bars: AUROC in cross-validation; whiskers: 95% range from resampling genes. The groups overlap (all three mark constrained regions), so their gains don't add up: all features together reach 0.958, against 0.904 for the ESM scores alone.

Inside the model

What the logistic regression relies on

The final model multiplies each feature, standardised to mean 0 and standard deviation 1, by a weight and adds the results. Positive weights push towards benign, negative towards pathogenic. Correlated features share credit (the raw ClinVar and gnomAD counts carry much of what the tolerance score carries), so read the top of the list as "what the model leans on", not as separate causes.

Design A

Why not predict the tolerance score directly?

The original plan was a model whose output is the tolerance score itself, learned from sequence, so it could score residues where no variant has been seen. Models were judged by the within-gene Spearman correlation between predicted and observed tolerance on the validation genes. Built twice from two random halves of the variants, the score agrees with itself only so far, which caps what any model can reach (the noise ceiling).

Within-gene Spearman correlation with the tolerance target (validation genes)

A cleaner target: count only pathogenic ClinVar variants on the ClinVar side

Try it in the gene explorer: set "ClinVar side" to "pathogenic only".

The target was mostly noise at single residues: the best model reached a third of the ceiling. Counting only pathogenic variants on the ClinVar side makes the target far more reproducible and more learnable, which is the most promising way back to Design A.

Context

How conserved are postsynaptic proteins?

Protein similarity of human genes to their orthologues (identical or conservative amino acids, positive BLOSUM62) and DNA identity in Ensembl's pairwise genome alignments, from chimpanzee to C. elegans (Ensembl 116). This is background for the poster, not a model input.

Bold number: median % similarity over genes with an orthologue (protein) or mean % identical bases over 80 regions (DNA). Small number: share of genes that have an orthologue. Hatched: fewer than 10% of genes have an orthologue, or Ensembl has no genome alignment for that species. All 86 ribosomal proteins are also in the postsynaptic set (SynGO annotates postsynaptic ribosomes).

Limits

What these results do not show yet

  • Three genes dominate the test. RYR1, ABL1 and VHL hold 52% of the test variants, and ESM-1v struggles in RYR1 and VHL. In the other 82 test genes, ESM-1v reaches 0.91 and the model 0.96: the same gap as in cross-validation. Try it in experiment 4.
  • The probabilities are not calibrated. At P(benign) ≥ 0.5, 97% of benign but only 52% of pathogenic test variants were classified correctly, mostly because of RYR1. Read the output as a ranking.
  • VUS are untested. The test used confidently classified variants. Whether the model picks out VUS that later turn out pathogenic needs a time-split test on an older ClinVar release.
  • Part of the signal reflects where labs look. ClinVar density is partly ascertainment; residue hiding, family splits and untouched genes limit, but don't remove, that effect.
  • gnomAD over-represents European ancestry, so some regions may look less tolerant than they are.

Glossary

AUROC
The chance that a random benign variant gets a more benign score than a random pathogenic one. 0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect.
Tolerance score
Smoothed gnomAD variant density minus smoothed ClinVar variant density, each scaled 0–1 within the gene. Above 0: more healthy variation than clinical reports (tolerated); below 0: intolerant.
ESM-1v LLR
log P(new amino acid) − log P(original) from the ESM-1v protein language model. Strongly negative means evolution rarely allows the swap.
VUS
Variant of uncertain significance: a ClinVar entry without a pathogenic or benign call.
RSA
Relative solvent accessibility: the share of a residue's surface exposed to water (core < 0.2, surface > 0.4).
pLDDT
AlphaFold's per-residue confidence (0–100); below 70 usually means a disordered region.
Disorder (AIUPred)
Predicted intrinsic disorder; residues scoring ≥ 0.5 are treated as unstructured.
Residue hiding
Building a variant's human-variant features without any variant at its own residue, so no variant informs its own score.
Cross-validation
Fitting on four fifths of the gene families and scoring the fifth, in rotation, so every variant is scored by a model that never saw its gene family.
Noise ceiling
The highest correlation any model could reach with a noisy target, estimated from how well the target agrees with itself.
Pre-registration
Fixing the analysis plan before looking at the test data.
